About

Dr Yanling Sun is a dancer, choreographer, educator, and dance researcher whose practice spans China and Aotearoa New Zealand. With nearly twenty years of training in Chinese classical dance, Chinese folk dance, and contemporary dance, her work explores how movement can carry cultural memory, connect generations, and open new ways of thinking through the body.

She is the first person to be awarded a Doctorate in Philosophy in Dance by the Division of Humanities at the University of Otago (2022–2025). Her doctoral research investigated the relationship between dance, installation art, and somatic practice, drawing on Sensory and Intuitive Ethnography across Aotearoa and Shanghai.

Her published articles have appeared in Research in Dance Education, Theatre, Dance and Performance Training, and Dance Research. She holds a master's degree in Dance Science and Education from the University of Edinburgh (Moray House), and a bachelor's in Dance Performance from Southwest Minzu University, China.

Her choreography has been performed at Allen Hall Theatre, New Athenaeum Theatre, Marama Hall (Ōtepoti Dunedin), as well as at the Shanghai Oriental Art Center, Poly Theatre, Jiangsu Grand Theatre, and Beijing Tianqiao Arts Center. She is committed to teaching, community dance, and cross-cultural performance.

Creative Practice

Featured Work · 2025

Sketches

Choreographer & Performer · Allen Hall Theatre, Ōtepoti Dunedin · Lighting Design by Martyn Roberts

A series of seven choreographic sketches exploring light, space, and embodied perception. Developed through somatic improvisation and spatial composition, the work treats movement as a living drawing process in which gesture, fabric, light, and shadow continually reconfigure one another. Sketches was Yanling's first full-length experimental dance theatre work, bringing together choreography, installation, lighting, and embodied storytelling.

Site-responsive · 2025

Saltlines for Sealion Woman

Performer · Wild Dunedin Festival of Nature · Choreography by Carol Brown · Caroline Plummer Fellowship

A site-specific movement work performed at the water's edge of St Clair Beach. Developed through the Caroline Plummer Community Dance Fellowship, the project responded to tide, sand, wind, and the pakakē (sea-lion) ecology — emphasising the body's reciprocal relationship with place rather than treating the environment as a passive backdrop.

Participatory Research · 2024

Doctoral Workshop — Wellington & Shanghai

Lead researcher & facilitator · Cross-cultural fieldwork

The first workshop in doctoral research was facilitated in Wellington with dancers from a Chinese-led studio. Participants were guided through somatic attention, movement improvisation, and spatial tasks that shifted emphasis away from externally imposed form and toward sensory awareness, choice, and responsive composition. The workshop formed part of a wider cross-cultural study conducted in Wellington, Ōtepoti Dunedin, and Shanghai.

Material & Site-responsive · 2025

Choreography as Trace, Environment, and Spatial Relation

From these and other works, choreography emerges as a way of tracing — between body and material, performer and environment, gesture and ground. Practice is developed through somatic improvisation, spatial composition, and the use of objects and site-specific prompts as concrete points of departure for curiosity and responsive composition.

Research

Investigating the intersection of dance, somatic practice, and installation art.

At the core of Yanling's research is a simple but generative question: how does somatic practice change the way choreography is made?

Through Sensory and Intuitive Ethnography, fieldwork with choreographers in Shanghai and dancers in Aotearoa, and the creation of sixteen practice-based workshops, her doctoral research explored how dancers connect more deeply with space, materials, and each other — and how ideas about the body travel across cultures. Bringing Eastern and Western perspectives together, the work opens new possibilities for dance making as an embodied inquiry.

"One of the most exciting parts of the research was that I was not only analysing dance works but also creating workshop-based choreographic processes with participants using different materials. It was both creative and research-based at the same time."

Research in Dance Education · 2025

The Cultural Flow of Monism in Dance: Somatic Practice and the Reshaping of Eastern Philosophical Embodiment in Chinese Dance

Yanling Sun

Taylor & Francis · Routledge · AHCI & SSCI · JCR Q1 (Dance Education Rank #1)

This article takes somatic practice as a starting point to explore the cultural flow of 'mind–body unity' across Eastern and Western contexts, focusing on its reception among Chinese dancers. Drawing on Sensory and Intuitive Ethnography, it investigates how Eastern-rooted concepts transform as they travel through Western education and back to China.

Theatre, Dance and Performance Training · 2026

Sensing Space, Moving with Intention: Reframing Choreographic Agency through Somatic and Installation Practices

Yanling Sun

Taylor & Francis · Routledge · AHCI & SSCI · JCR Q2 (Arts & Humanities)

This article reframes choreographic agency through the lens of somatic and installation practices. It argues that dancers' sensing of space and material intention reshapes how authorship is distributed between body, environment, and the collaborators in a choreographic process.

Community & Teaching

Chinese dance teaching in Aotearoa and intergenerational performance.

Chinese Aotearoa

Across 2024 and 2025, Yanling taught Chinese dance in community settings around Ōtepoti Dunedin and Christchurch, adapting movement vocabulary and cultural context for local learners. The sustained teaching relationship strengthened her understanding of how Chinese dance is transmitted, negotiated, and made meaningful within everyday life in Aotearoa.

Spring Blossoms (Chun Hua)

Choreographer · Lan Yuan Dunedin Chinese Garden · Lunar New Year 2025

Created for young performers from the local Chinese community, the work used group choreography to connect seasonal celebration, cultural learning, and public performance, foregrounding children as active participants in the transmission and reinterpretation of cultural knowledge.

A Great River (Yi Tiao Da He)

Choreographer / Rehearsal Director · Mid-Autumn Festival 2025

Presented at Lan Yuan Dunedin Chinese Garden and invited to a Mid-Autumn Festival event associated with the Consulate-General of the People's Republic of China in Christchurch. The work brought dance, music, and community gathering together around ideas of memory, homeland, and belonging.
